Climate change and environmental issues have drawn growing global attention in recent years, and businesses face increasing pressure to cut greenhouse gas emissions and pursue more sustainable operations. As a globally recognized tech giant, Apple stands out not only for product innovation but also for its active environmental efforts to reduce carbon emissions. In this article, we look at Apple's concrete measures on greenhouse gas emissions from an accountant's perspective, and how they align with the requirements of the GHG Protocol.

## Scope 1 — Direct Emissions

First, let's look at Apple's direct emissions measures. Under the GHG Protocol, this covers emissions from Apple's own facilities and data centers. Apple **actively uses renewable energy**, such as solar and wind power, to meet the electricity needs of these facilities. This not only helps cut emissions, but also translates in accounting terms to lower energy costs and reduced environmental risk.

## Scope 2 — Purchased Electricity Emissions

Here, Apple has taken another important step: it participates in renewable energy procurement programs to ensure the electricity it purchases comes from renewable sources, further reducing the emissions embedded in its purchased power. This benefits the environment and supports the company's sustainable development, as more consumers and investors pay attention to corporate environmental policy.

## Scope 3 — Other Indirect Emissions

In this category, Apple uses several methods to reduce indirect emissions across its products.

First, it uses recycled and renewable materials, which typically have a lower carbon footprint than virgin materials, helping to cut product-related emissions.

Apple also uses recyclable packaging materials and plans to achieve plastic-free packaging, further reducing indirect emissions.

For transport emissions, Apple actively improves shipping methods — for example, favoring ocean and rail freight over air freight — to reduce the carbon footprint of transporting its products.

**Carbon Offset Program**:

Finally, it's worth noting that when emissions can't be fully eliminated through these efforts, Apple plans to rely on high-quality carbon offset programs, supporting conservation and restoration projects to offset remaining emissions. This reflects corporate responsibility and environmental awareness, and in accounting terms represents the execution of environmental obligations.

In summary, as a globally recognized company, Apple has actively adopted a range of measures to address climate change and reduce greenhouse gas emissions. These measures not only help protect the environment but also reflect corporate social responsibility and a commitment to sustainable development, contributing to the company's long-term financial stability. It's a good example of how a company can balance accounting and environmental goals to achieve sustainable operations.
