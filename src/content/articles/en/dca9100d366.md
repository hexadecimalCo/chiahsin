In Taiwan, beyond income tax to support the national social security system, individuals with certain types of income must also pay a supplementary premium to help fund additional social welfare and healthcare benefits. This article explains how the supplementary premium is calculated, its thresholds and caps, with worked examples.

## How the Supplementary Premium Is Calculated

The supplementary premium is calculated differently depending on the income type. Here are the common categories and how each is calculated:

**1. Bonuses exceeding 4x the monthly insured salary (cumulative annual amount):**

In this case, the portion of the bonus exceeding 4x the monthly insured salary is subject to a 2.11% premium rate. Example:

Suppose someone receives a NT$10,000 bonus in a given month, with a monthly insured salary of NT$2,000. The amount exceeding the 4x threshold is (NT$10,000 − NT$2,000) = NT$8,000, so the supplementary premium is NT$8,000 × 2.11% = NT$168.80.

**2. Part-time salary income:**

If someone's part-time salary income reaches the minimum wage announced by the central labor authority, a 2.11% premium applies. Assuming the announced minimum wage is NT$30,000 and someone's part-time income is NT$40,000, the supplementary premium is NT$40,000 × 2.11% = NT$844.00.

**3. Professional practice income:**

Once professional practice income reaches NT$20,000, a 2.11% premium applies. If someone earns NT$25,000 in a single transaction, the supplementary premium is NT$25,000 × 2.11% = NT$527.50.

**4. Dividend income:**

Dividend income is calculated somewhat differently, depending on the insured party's status and insured amount. Examples:

For those insured as an employer or self-employed business owner, once a single dividend payment exceeds the amount already factored into their insured salary by NT$20,000, a 2.11% premium applies to the excess. For example, if someone insured as an employer receives a NT$25,000 dividend, and NT$10,000 was already factored into their insured amount, the excess is (NT$25,000 − NT$10,000) = NT$15,000, so the supplementary premium is NT$15,000 × 2.11% = NT$316.50.

For those not insured as an employer or self-employed business owner, once a single payment reaches NT$20,000, a 2.11% premium applies.

**5. Interest income:**

Interest income is also subject to a 2.11% premium, once a single payment reaches NT$20,000 or more. For example, if someone receives NT$25,000 in interest, the supplementary premium is NT$25,000 × 2.11% = NT$527.50.

**6. Rental income:**

Like interest income, rental income is subject to a 2.11% supplementary premium once a single payment reaches NT$20,000 or more.

### Caps and Thresholds on the Supplementary Premium

It's also worth noting that the supplementary premium is subject to certain caps:

- For bonuses exceeding 4x the monthly insured salary (cumulative annual amount), there is no explicit cap.
- For part-time salary income, the cap is a single payment of no more than NT$10 million.
- The cap for professional practice income, dividend income, interest income, and rental income is also a single payment of no more than NT$10 million.
